# Can a content policy be rewritten so reviewers agree more often?

A trust & safety policy study for **Corkboard**, a fictional neighborhood app. Three AI
reviewers (Claude Haiku 5.5, Claude Sonnet 5.5, Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite) labeled 480 reported
posts under three versions of the same enforcement policy. Agreement between reviewers was
measured with Fleiss' kappa, the policy was revised where reviewers split, and every revision
was tested on posts it had never been tuned against.

## Findings

1. **Policy wording, not reviewer skill, drove most disagreement.** On clear-cut posts the three
   reviewers agreed almost perfectly (kappa 0.87–1.00). On borderline posts they split 30–40%
   of the time, and the splits clustered on questions the policy never answered: who counts as
   "identifiable", whether a friendly post can still leak a phone number, what makes a listing
   "suspicious".

2. **Privacy improved the most, and the gain held on unseen posts.** Defining consent (assume
   none unless stated, except household members and business contacts) and saying that license
   plates and home photos are removed *for any purpose* raised privacy agreement from **0.30 to
   0.63** on the final clean set (95% CI for the change: +0.09 to +0.60).

3. **Adding a middle option made harassment worse.** v1's harassment rules had two answers,
   REMOVE or ALLOW. v2 added LIMIT for "shaming someone's conduct without an insult". On new
   posts, reviewers used it as a compromise for posts that clearly insulted people ("the worst
   person on this street", mocking a 12-year-old who fell at a parade). Harassment agreement
   fell from **0.92 to 0.71** (95% CI −0.42 to −0.03). A line v3 added ("LIMIT is not a
   compromise") did not fix it. **Recommendation: remove LIMIT from the harassment section.**

4. **A policy tuned on the posts you read looks better than it is.** v2 raised agreement from
   0.71 to 0.83 on the 240 posts used to write it, but only from 0.79 to 0.81 on 120 new ones.
   Every revision here was therefore judged on a set it had never seen.

5. **Disagreement finds errors in the answer key, too.** On one post (a stranger's phone number
   shared with hostility) two of three reviewers chose ESCALATE while the key said REMOVE. The
   policy's own text required ESCALATE; the key was wrong. Four key corrections are recorded in
   `data/posts.json` with a reason for each.

## Results

Fleiss' kappa across the three reviewers (1 = perfect agreement, 0 = chance). Each set was
used for exactly one purpose:

| Set | Used to | v1 | v2 | v3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dev (240 posts) | write v2 | 0.71 | 0.83 | 0.83 |
| Holdout 1 (120) | test v2, then write v3 | 0.79 | 0.81 | *0.82, tuned* |
| **Holdout 2 (120)** | **final test of all three** | **0.67** | **0.73** | **0.75** |

Holdout 2 by area (20 posts each; one post moves an area's numbers noticeably):

| Area | v1 | v2 | v3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Privacy | 0.30 | 0.51 | **0.63** |
| Scams | 0.70 | 0.78 | 0.83 |
| Misinformation | 0.73 | 0.75 | 0.77 |
| Spam | 0.65 | 0.85 | 0.80 |
| Impersonation | 0.68 | 0.72 | 0.68 |
| Harassment | **0.92** | 0.76 | 0.71 |
| Borderline posts only | 0.53 | 0.63 | 0.61 |

Overall v1 → v3 on holdout 2: **+0.07** (95% CI −0.01 to +0.15; the bootstrap puts a 95%
chance on a real improvement). v2 → v3: +0.02, within noise.

## What changed in each version

**v2** (`data/policy_v2.md`, changes marked ▲), written from the 67 dev posts that split:
- A "before you apply a section" block: check every section, define *identifiable person*,
  allow obvious jokes, judge posts as written because reviewers cannot verify facts.
- Harassment: conditional and implied threats, wishing harm, and calls to "deal with" someone
  are placed explicitly; officials may be mocked for decisions but not for appearance.
- Privacy: consent is assumed absent; friendly tone does not make a phone number shareable;
  welfare-check posts go to the Safety team.
- Misinformation: contradicting an active official warning is a removal; "worked on all three
  of my kids" does not exempt dangerous advice; unsourced claims that a service changed are
  limited.
- Scams: a concrete red-flag list for LIMIT and a narrow definition of REMOVE.
- Spam: "local" means within two miles or serving the neighborhood; tag/comment-to-win is
  engagement bait.

**v3** (changes marked ◆), written from holdout 1, where v2 created new problems:
- The consent rule had started removing a lost-dog post because it included the husband's
  number, and a recommendation for a lawn-care business run from home. Household members and
  business contacts are now exceptions.
- "LIMIT is not a compromise": use it only when no removal condition applies.
- Insults inside a conduct complaint ("jerk") are removals; birthday roasts are allowed.

## Method

- **Posts** were generated by Claude Opus 5.5 from the v1 policy, 40% clear and 60%
  borderline, with an intended action and a reason for each. Both holdout sets were generated
  after the version they test was frozen, and were never read while writing it.
- **Reviewers** saw the policy and batches of 20 posts in a fixed shuffled order, never the
  post's area. Each returned an action, the policy section applied, and a confidence of 1–3.
- **Agreement**: Fleiss' kappa across all three reviewers, plus pairwise Cohen's kappa. Kappa
  is the primary measure because it does not depend on the answer key.
- **Uncertainty**: paired bootstrap over posts (2,000 resamples) for each version-to-version
  change (`bootstrap.py`).
- **Cost**: 4,320 labels for $2.36 at list prices: per 1,000 posts, $1.36 for Sonnet,
  $0.14 for Haiku and $0.15 for Gemini.

## Limitations

- **The reviewers are AI models, not trained human moderators.** Models agree with each other
  more than people do on clear cases and may split differently on hard ones. The study
  measures policy clarity *for these reviewers*.
- **Posts and answer keys come from one model** writing to the v1 policy, so accuracy against
  the key favors v1. That is why agreement, not accuracy, is the headline number.
- **20 posts per area per holdout set** is small. Area-level changes under about 0.2 kappa
  should not be read as real; the two that clear that bar (privacy up, harassment down) are
  reported with intervals.
- **One labeling run per version.** Run-to-run variation is not measured.

## Reproduce

Python 3.9+, standard library only. Needs the `claude` CLI and a Gemini API key.

    python3 data/generate_posts.py                       # dev set, 240 posts
    python3 label.py --policy v1 --set posts             # also v2, v3; --set holdout / holdout2
    python3 analyze.py --policy v1 --set posts           # reports/<set>_<version>_summary.json + disagreements
    python3 data/generate_posts.py --set holdout2 --n 20 # a fresh test set
    python3 bootstrap.py --set holdout2 --a v1 --b v3 [--area privacy]

| Path | What it is |
|---|---|
| `data/policy_v1.md` … `policy_v3.md` | The three policy versions, changes marked |
| `data/posts.json`, `holdout.json`, `holdout2.json` | The posts, intended actions and reasons |
| `labels/<set>/<version>/<model>/` | Every raw label with section and confidence |
| `reports/<set>_<version>_disagreements.md` | Every post the reviewers split on, with votes |
