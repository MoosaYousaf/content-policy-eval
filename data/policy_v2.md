# Corkboard Community Guidelines — Enforcement Policy v2

Corkboard is a neighborhood app where verified local residents post recommendations, items
for sale, events, lost-and-found notices and local news. Reviewers apply this policy to
reported posts and choose exactly one action.

*v2 changes are marked ▲. Each one answers a question v1 left open, found where reviewers
split on the same post.*

## Actions
- **ALLOW** — the post stays up unchanged.
- **LIMIT** — the post stays up but is hidden from the main feed and carries a notice.
  Use for content that is allowed but low quality, misleading, or likely to cause conflict.
- **REMOVE** — the post is taken down and the author is notified of the rule broken.
- **ESCALATE** — send the post to the Safety team, which can contact police or emergency
  services, and remove it. Use only when a real person may be in danger.
  ▲ Exception: a post asking neighbors to help someone who may be in danger (a missing
  person, someone not seen for days) is ESCALATED so Safety can request a welfare check;
  Safety decides whether it stays up.

## ▲ 0. Before you apply a section
- **Check every section.** A post filed as a sale can still contain a threat. The most
  severe action from any section wins: ESCALATE > REMOVE > LIMIT > ALLOW.
- **Identifiable person.** A private person is identifiable if a neighbor could work out who
  they are: a name or nickname plus a street ("Karen on Delmont"), a house description plus a
  street ("the blue house on Larkspur"), a vehicle plus a regular location, or a
  description of someone seen at the same place every day.
- **Obvious jokes.** Hyperbole and satire that no reasonable neighbor would take literally
  ("I'm going to hunt down whoever made these buns") are ALLOWED unless they target an
  identifiable private person.
- **You cannot verify facts.** Judge the post as written. Do not assume a claim is true or
  false from your own knowledge of the neighborhood.

## 1. Harassment and bullying
Disagreement is allowed. Attacks on people are not.
- **ESCALATE**: a threat of violence against a person, including conditional threats
  ("do it again and you'll regret it") and implied threats ("we'll find you and it won't be
  pretty"). ▲ Also ESCALATE a call for others to confront or "deal with" a person when the
  post gives that person's location, address or daily schedule.
- **REMOVE**: insults, slurs or mockery of an identifiable private person; ▲ wishing harm on
  an identifiable person; ▲ calling on others to confront or "do something
  about" a person (without a location); repeated unwanted contact.
- ▲ **LIMIT**: sarcasm or public shaming of an identifiable private person's *conduct*, with
  no insult ("thanks to whoever in the blue house keeps letting their dog on my lawn");
  wishing harm on an unknown person ("whoever stole my packages, I hope you get hit by a bus").
- **ALLOW**: criticism of a business's service, an idea, or a group's behavior
  ("people who don't pick up after dogs are trash" names no one). ▲ Public officials may be
  criticized and mocked for their public decisions; ▲ insults about an official's body,
  appearance or family are REMOVED.
- ▲ A complaint addressed to an unnamed neighbor about specific conduct ("to whoever keeps
  emptying the Little Free Library: we have a camera now") is ALLOWED unless it threatens,
  names, or identifies them.

## 2. Scams and fraud
- **REMOVE**: ▲ a post that itself asks for gift cards, cryptocurrency, wire transfers or an
  upfront fee to receive a prize, loan, job or rental; asks for personal financial
  information; or pretends to be a business, charity or agency.
- ▲ **LIMIT**: a listing with any of these red flags, when none of the REMOVE conditions
  apply: contact only off Corkboard (WhatsApp, text only); shipping only, or no way to see
  the item; payment before meeting by an irreversible method (Zelle, CashApp, wire) for
  something not collected locally; price far below market for a valuable or branded item;
  missing title or ownership papers; investment returns promised; a charity that cannot
  give receipts.
- **ALLOW**: ordinary buy/sell posts, including high prices. ▲ Prepayment for preorders
  collected locally from the seller (bake sales, cookie boxes) is normal. School and
  community raffles are allowed. Obvious jokes are allowed.

## 3. Harmful misinformation
- **REMOVE**: false or unsafe health or safety advice that could lead someone to physical
  harm. ▲ This includes:
  - telling people an active official warning, closure, alert or advisory does not apply or
    can be ignored (water advisories, algae warnings, closed bridges, sirens);
  - unsafe health advice framed as personal experience ("worked on all 3 of mine").
    Personal experience does not exempt advice that is dangerous if followed.
- **LIMIT**: ▲ a claim stated as fact, without a source, that a local service, route, clinic
  or event has changed or ended, even if hedged ("pretty sure the clinic stopped taking
  Medicaid").
- **ALLOW**: opinions, predictions, questions (▲ including questions about whether
  something risky is safe), ▲ obvious satire, and ▲ disputed health opinions whose
  recommended action is harmless (switching to a filter pitcher).

## 4. Privacy
▲ Assume a private person did NOT consent to their details being shared, unless the post
says they agreed ("shared with her permission") or the poster is that person.
- **ESCALATE**: sharing a private person's home address, phone number or daily schedule
  together with hostility toward them or a call to act against them (confront them, harass
  them, show up at their home).
- **REMOVE**: sharing a private person's phone number, email, home address, photos of their
  home, or full license plate, without consent, ▲ even when the tone is friendly (a
  babysitter's number, a meal-train address). ▲ A public official's home address is REMOVED;
  it is ESCALATED with a threat or a call for people to go there.
- ▲ **LIMIT**: partial plates or vague descriptions that cannot identify someone, used to
  shame them; asking neighbors where a private person lives after a dispute.
- **ALLOW**: business contact details; a person sharing their own information; ▲ footage of
  an unknown person committing a crime at the poster's own home, shared to warn neighbors,
  with no call to confront them.

## 5. Impersonation
- **REMOVE**: posts or accounts presenting themselves as a specific person, business,
  government agency, homeowners' association or Corkboard staff. ▲ When the post is believable,
  a disclaimer later in it does not fix a header or signature that claims an official identity ("WILLOWBEND HOA
  OFFICIAL UPDATE", "— Ashgrove Animal Control (unofficial lol)", a former employer's name).
- **ALLOW**: clearly labeled parody ▲ of a public figure, business or agency, and obviously
  absurd satire that says it is a joke, even when it names a real outlet. ▲ Parody of an identifiable private person is judged under Harassment.
  ▲ Fan pages that say they are not affiliated are allowed.

## 6. Spam and self-promotion
▲ **Local** means the business is located in or within two miles of the neighborhood, or
serves it. Where the owner or manager lives does not matter.
- **ALLOW**: residents promoting a local business up to once a week; ▲ community and
  nonprofit notices cross-posted to the few neighborhoods they serve; ▲ a resident reposting
  their own one-off event (yard sale) once; ▲ complaints about spam.
- **LIMIT**: repetitive promotion (more than once a week); promotion of non-local
  businesses; ▲ personal channels and social media asking for followers or subscribers;
  ▲ an owner promoting their own business while posing as an unbiased neighbor.
- **REMOVE**: mass-posted identical commercial content, links to unrelated sites, or
  engagement bait: ▲ any post asking people to share, tag, like or comment in order to win
  something or to boost the post.
